The question

Indian cities send most of their solid waste to open dumpsites. Many are large, few are mapped, and all of them can be seen from space. The study that prompted this project reported finding hundreds of such sites in one state from free satellite data, using a few thresholds, but never checked what its detections were.

{{note:sentinel2}}

This project asks a narrower question and checks the answer. If a model is trained on dumpsites elsewhere in India, does it find the dumpsites of Gujarat, a state it has never seen? And when it is wrong, why?

{{note:heldout}}

One row of data is one 10 m square of ground, described by 26 numbers measured by the {{term:sentinel2|Sentinel-2}}, {{term:landsat|Landsat}} and {{term:srtm|SRTM}} missions, and labelled landfill or not landfill. The model reads those 26 numbers and returns a {{term:score|score}} between 0 and 1.

The answer in five numbers

{{k:guj.sites_found}} of {{k:guj.sites}}known Gujarat dumpsites found
{{k:guj.share_flagged}}of the land scored was flagged, as {{k:guj.alarms}} separate alarm patches
{{k:new.sites_found}} of {{k:new.sites}}dumpsites found in six further cities, sealed before scoring
{{k:check.dumps_new}}dumpsites not known before, found among the 100 strongest alarms
{{k:val.f1}}F1 outside Gujarat, with whole regions held out

The model recognises a dumpsite when it is shown one: it flags at least half of the outline of {{k:guj.sites_found}} of the {{k:guj.sites}} sites on a list that was fixed in advance. It also flags {{k:guj.km2_flagged}} km² of other land. The known sites are {{k:guj.site_px_share_of_flagged}} of what it flags.

A second blind test gave the same picture: in six more cities, whose dumpsites were outlined and committed before their land was scored, it found {{k:new.sites_found}} of {{k:new.sites}}. Its 100 strongest alarms, checked by eye, led to {{k:check.dumps_new}} dumpsites that were not known beforehand.

The model's weakness is the amount of other land it flags. Most of the checked alarms were {{k:check.top_seen}} and similar surfaces. The rest of this site shows how these numbers were obtained, what the model confuses with a dumpsite, and why.

How the study was run

  1. Label. {{k:train.places}} dumpsites in {{k:train.states}} states and territories outside Gujarat were outlined by hand from high-resolution imagery, with {{k:train.lookalike_places}} look-alike places (quarries, ash ponds, bare ground). Data
  2. Measure. 26 features were read for {{k:train.points}} sample pixels from free satellite archives. Data
  3. Compare. Nine models, from a published threshold rule to gradient boosting, were scored with every place in one region of India held out in turn. Method
  4. Freeze. The best model, its threshold and the scoring rules were committed under a Git tag before Gujarat was looked at. Method
  5. Test. The frozen model scored {{k:guj.pixels_scored_m}} pixels around {{k:guj.cities}} Gujarat cities. Its 100 strongest alarms were then judged one by one, without their scores. Results
  6. Diagnose. The errors were traced back to the features. Diagnosis

What to know before reading

This is the third model built in this project. Two earlier versions were trained, frozen and scored on the same {{k:guj.sites}} Gujarat sites, and what they got wrong shaped this one. The Gujarat results here are therefore not a first-time test. No Gujarat data was used to train any version. The earlier versions are summarised under How we got here.
