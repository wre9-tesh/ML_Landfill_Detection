{{note:gbm}}

All learned models use scikit-learn defaults with balanced class weights, a threshold of 0.5 and random seed 678. Nothing was tuned.

Why no tuning: with {{k:train.places}} places in six folds, tuned settings would fit the folds. Fixed defaults keep the comparison between models fair and leave nothing to adjust after seeing a result.
Why a threshold rule is the baseline: it is the method of the study that prompted the project. If three thresholds were enough, nothing more complicated would be justified.

Three ways to split the data

The same models were validated three ways. Only the third resembles the real task.

{{note:leakage}}
  • Random pixel. Points are assigned to folds at random, so pixels from the same dumpsite land on both sides. A model can score well by recognising the site, not the class.
  • Held-out site. Whole {{term:place|places}} are held out. Neighbouring sites in the same city or state remain in training.
  • Held-out region (the headline). Every place in one of India's six {{term:region|Zonal Council regions}} is held out in turn. The model is tested on a part of the country it has not seen, which is what scoring Gujarat demands.
{{note:region}}
Why regions and not states: the plan was to hold out one state at a time, for states with at least ten places. Only Maharashtra has that many, so the fallback written into the plan applied: six regions of 5 to 23 places each.

Metrics and the selection rule

{{note:prf}}

For each held-out region: precision, recall and F1 for the landfill class at a threshold of 0.5. They are reported as the mean and standard deviation over the six regions. The share of each background class that is flagged is reported alongside, because a single precision figure hides which kind of land causes the false alarms.

The model with the highest mean F1 on the held-out-region split is selected. This rule was written down before the models were run.

The freeze

A test is only a test if nothing can be adjusted after seeing it. Three things were fixed in the repository's history, in this order:

  1. 11 November. The Gujarat test list was committed (8711855), before any model existed.
  2. 15 November. The selected model, its threshold and every scoring rule were committed and tagged freeze-v3. The rules are in docs/freeze_v3.md, including what would count as an improvement.
  3. After the tag. Gujarat was scored. The scoring script refuses to run unless the model file is the tagged one.
{{note:freeze}}

This is the third model built in this project. Two earlier versions were frozen and scored on the same {{k:guj.sites}} sites, so the Gujarat results are not a first-time test. See How we got here.

The hand check

Recall on {{k:guj.sites}} known sites says nothing about the thousands of other places the model flags. To measure that, the 100 strongest alarm patches were given random identifiers and opened one at a time in Google Earth, with scores and ranks hidden. Each was recorded as a dump, a look-alike or unsure, with a note of what was there.

{{note:handcheck}}

One hundred is enough to estimate a proportion to within about ten percentage points. The check was done by the author alone, from imagery, which makes it the weakest link in the evaluation: a second checker or a field visit could disagree.
