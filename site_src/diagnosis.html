The model scores well in validation, finds the known sites, and is wrong about most of what it flags. This page takes that apart as six questions. Each ends with a one-sentence answer.

1. What did the model flag that is not a dumpsite?

In the hand check, {{k:check.lookalikes}} of the 100 strongest alarms were not dumpsites. {{k:check.seen.bare_soil}} were bare soil or open plots, {{k:check.seen.field_or_grass}} were fields, grass or scrub, {{k:check.seen.building}} were buildings and {{k:check.seen.industrial_plant}} were industrial plants.

Answer: mostly bare, dry ground, with or without buildings on it.

2. Can landfill pixels be told apart at all?

Before blaming the model, look at the data without one. Three views.

One variable at a time. The table gives the {{term:auc|AUC}} of each variable for landfill against each background class. Against ordinary land, the temperature anomaly alone reaches {{k:diag.auc_lstanom_random}}. Against quarries it drops to {{k:diag.auc_lstanom_quarry}} and against bare soil to {{k:diag.auc_lstanom_bare_soil}}. Against bare soil, most of the other variables sit between 0.4 and 0.6.

{{note:auc}}

All variables together. For each point, take its 15 most similar points from other places and ask how many are landfill. {{k:diag.nn_landfill}} of landfill points sit in a landfill-majority neighbourhood. So do {{k:diag.nn_random}} of random background points, {{k:diag.nn_bare}} of bare-soil points and {{k:diag.nn_quarry}} of quarry points.

{{note:purity}}

Two dimensions. The first two {{term:pca|principal components}} hold {{k:diag.pc12}} of the variation. Landfill points form one cloud. Ordinary land overlaps its edge; bare soil and quarries sit inside it.

Answer: yes against ordinary land, only partly against bare ground and quarries.

3. Which look-alikes fool the model?

When a region is held out, the model flags {{k:diag.fa_landfill}} of its landfill points, which is the recall. It also flags {{k:diag.fa_quarry}} of quarry points, {{k:diag.fa_bare}} of bare-soil points and {{k:diag.fa_ash}} of ash-pond points, against {{k:diag.fa_random}} of random background and {{k:diag.fa_near}} of the background near landfills.

Answer: all three kinds, quarries most. Validation already showed the weakness that the Gujarat alarms expose.

4. What does the model rely on?

{{term:shap|SHAP}} values divide each prediction among the features. Summed over training points, the two thermal variables carry {{k:diag.shap_thermal}} of the model's decisions, and one of them, the temperature anomaly, carries {{k:diag.top_feature_share}} alone. Pre-monsoon colour carries {{k:diag.shap_premonsoon}}, the winter season {{k:diag.shap_winter}} and the surroundings {{k:diag.shap_surroundings}}.

{{note:shap}}

Answer: chiefly on temperature. Anything dry, dark and bare is warmer than its surroundings in May, whether or not it is waste.

5. Why were the Gujarat alarms flagged?

There are two ways for a model to be wrong in a new place. The pixels may be unlike anything in training, so the model is guessing ({{term:novelty|extrapolation}}). Or the pixels may closely resemble training landfills, so the model is doing what it learned and the features cannot tell the difference. The two call for different remedies, so the 26 features were read at the centre of each top-100 alarm and each known site.

{{note:novelty}}

The false alarms are not unusual. None lies beyond the 99th percentile of the training data; their median is the {{k:alarm.la_novelty}} percentile. For {{k:alarm.la_nearest_landfill}} of the {{k:alarm.la_n}}, the most similar training points are landfill.

What differs is where the score comes from. For the false alarms the thermal variables contribute {{k:alarm.la_shap_thermal}} and colour, winter and surroundings together {{k:alarm.la_shap_other}}. For the known sites the split is {{k:alarm.site_shap_thermal}} and {{k:alarm.site_shap_other}}, and for alarms that proved to be dumps {{k:alarm.dump_shap_thermal}} and {{k:alarm.dump_shap_other}}. A real dumpsite is supported by several kinds of evidence; a false alarm is carried over the threshold by temperature almost alone.

Answer: not extrapolation. In these features, hot bare ground in Gujarat is a close match for training landfills.

6. Why does a good F1 give thousands of alarms?

Because F1 was measured where landfill is common and the model was used where it is rare. In the training table one point in nine is landfill. In the scored part of Gujarat the known sites are one pixel in {{k:rare.sites_one_in}}.

{{note:prevalence}}

Hold the model's behaviour fixed: it flags {{k:diag.fa_landfill}} of landfill pixels and {{k:diag.fa_random}} of ordinary ones. At one landfill pixel in nine, about three of every four flagged pixels are landfill. At one in a thousand, {{k:rare.precision_1000}} are. Nothing about the model has changed; there is simply far more ordinary land to be wrong about. In Gujarat the measured false-alarm rate was higher still, {{k:rare.fa_gujarat}}.

Answer: a false-alarm rate of a few percent is small in a table and enormous on a map.

The missed site

Rajkot's dumpsite is the only one of the {{k:guj.sites}} not found: {{k:guj.missed.share}} of its outline is flagged, and the highest score inside it is {{k:guj.missed.max_score}}. The model does respond to the site, but not over half of it. Why the rest was not flagged has not been established.

How this compares with published work

The study that prompted this project (Bhajantri and Mandpe, 2026) applied three thresholds across Madhya Pradesh and reported sites that met them, without checking what those places were. The one study found that verified Sentinel-2 detections by people (Kruse and others, 2023) confirmed about half of its candidates, using a pixel network, a patch network, imagery from two dates, retraining on its own false alarms and human review. Against that background, a low confirmation rate from a pixel model with no tuning is not surprising. The full citations are on the Reproduce page.

Limitations

  1. Third model on the first test list. The {{k:guj.sites}} sites were scored by two earlier versions (next section), so that recall is not a first-time result. The six new cities are a first-time test, but of only {{k:new.sites}} sites.
  2. One person, from imagery. The training labels, the test list and the hand check are all the author's reading of satellite images. No site was visited and no second person checked.
  3. The test list favours large municipal sites. Small informal dumps were not tested. Sites under 1 ha were excluded by design.
  4. Most of the state was not scored. The boxes cover {{k:guj.share_of_state}} of Gujarat and were drawn around known sites. Unscored land is unknown, not clean.
  5. The hand check covers the top 100 of {{k:guj.alarms}} alarms. It says nothing about the rest; {{k:check.dumps}} dumpsites among {{k:check.judged}} judged alarms is a small sample (95% interval {{k:check.ci_low}} to {{k:check.ci_high}}).
  6. One year. All imagery is from late 2023 to mid 2024. Dumpsites open, grow and are remediated.
  7. Small training set. {{k:train.places}} places and three look-alike classes.
  8. No tuning and no deep model. A network that reads image patches might separate a dumpsite from a bare plot by its shape and setting. This project did not test one and cannot say.
  9. Features describe pixels. Even the surroundings features summarise 50 to 150 m around a pixel. Nothing describes a site as a whole: its access road, its edge, its distance from a town.

How we got here

The model on this site is the third of three. The first, a logistic regression on 12 single-date features, found 16 of the 17 sites, but none of 50 hand-checked alarms was a dumpsite: 33 were blue metal roofs. The second added 25,800 background points from around the training landfills; a random forest then flagged far less land, but ranked large bare patches first and was judged a failure by eye. The third added a winter composite and the pixel's surroundings. Each was frozen under its own Git tag before Gujarat was scored, and no Gujarat pixel or hand-check verdict was ever used in training.
