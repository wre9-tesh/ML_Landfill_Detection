What one row is

A training row is one 10 m pixel at a sampled point. It has an identifier, a label (1 for landfill, 0 for anything else), a class (landfill, near-site background, random background, bare soil, quarry or ash pond), the place and state it belongs to, and 26 feature values.

A Gujarat row is one 10 m pixel inside a scored box, with the same 26 features and no label. There are 129 million of them. Results for Gujarat are not reported per pixel but per known site and per alarm patch, because a site is what an inspector would visit.

Everything the model reads is free and global. The exact collection names, dates and cloud limits are stored with each feature table (features_meta.json) and are the same for the training points and for Gujarat.

How the labels were made

There is no public list of Indian dumpsites with outlines. Two sources were combined.

OpenStreetMap. 843 places tagged as landfill outside Gujarat were put in a fixed order. 107 were screened by eye in the time available. Only 13 were active dumpsites with a usable outline; 93 were dropped. 60 of the dropped places were kept as look-alike examples: 32 ash ponds, 16 bare-soil sites and 12 quarries that someone had mapped as landfill.

Look-alike. A place that is not a dump but resembles one from above: a quarry, an ash pond, bare ground. Used in training as a hard example of what not to flag.

Hand-drawn outlines. 82 further dumpsites were found and outlined in Google Earth. After merging outlines within 500 m of each other and removing sites under 1 ha, 86 places remain (11 from OpenStreetMap, 75 hand-drawn) in 26 states and territories.

The rule for a site was fixed before labelling began: dumping must be visibly active in the image closest to March to May 2024, and the visible waste must cover at least 1 ha.

Size

Points were drawn inside the outlines: up to 50 per landfill place and up to 30 per look-alike place. As many random points as landfill points were drawn across the same states, and 25,800 more background points were drawn in the 15 km boxes around the training landfills. Of 35,330 points, 985 lacked at least one feature and were dropped, leaving 34,345. Landfill is 11% of them.

Near-site background. Points drawn at random from the 15 km box around each training landfill. They show the model what the land around a city dump looks like.

The test set is separate: 17 known dumpsites in 13 Gujarat cities, 313 ha in all. The list was committed to the repository (commit 8711855, 11 November) before any model was run, and nothing from Gujarat is in the training table.

Sealed test list. The list of known Gujarat dumpsites with their outlines, committed before any model was run and not used in training.

The 26 variables

The variables fall into six groups. The first three (12 variables) describe one pixel on one date. The last three (14 variables) add a second season and the pixel's surroundings.

NDVI. A greenness index from the red and near-infrared bands. High on crops and trees, near zero on bare ground, waste and roofs.

Quality problems

  1. Labels are one person's reading of imagery. No site was visited. The high-resolution image used for labelling is not always from the same months as the satellite features.
  2. Few places, unevenly spread. 86 places; 15 are in Maharashtra and 13 states or territories have two or fewer. This is why validation holds out regions, not states.
  3. OpenStreetMap is unreliable for this purpose. 13 of 107 screened outlines were usable, and 736 candidates were never screened.
  4. Look-alike examples cover three classes. Quarries, ash ponds and bare soil are represented. Buildings, cropland after harvest and industrial yards, which turned out to matter in Gujarat, are represented only by whatever the background points happened to hit.
  5. Temperature is coarse. The thermal band has pixels of about 100 m, resampled to 10 m. A small site is blurred into its surroundings, which is one reason sites under 1 ha were excluded.
  6. Missing values. 985 of 35,330 points (2.8%) were dropped: snow in Himalayan states, gaps in the winter composite, and points with no temperature reading.
  7. The test list is small and not random. 17 sites in 13 cities, compiled from public lists and the author's own knowledge. It favours large municipal sites.
  8. Most of Gujarat was not scored. The boxes cover 6.6% of the state.
