Setup

Python 3.13 on Windows PowerShell (the scripts also run on Linux and macOS):

python -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt

The package versions used for training are pinned in requirements.txt (scikit-learn 1.9.1).

Run order

Steps marked EE need a free Google Earth Engine account and project. Every other step runs from files in the repository.

#CommandWrites
1python scripts/build_samples.pydata/samples/points.csv
2python scripts/build_background_v2.pydata/samples/points_v2_background.csv
3 (EE)python scripts/gee_features.py --project ID, and again with --points data/samples/points_v2_background.csv --out data/features_v2The 12 base features
4 (EE)The same two fetches with --stack extra and --out data/features_v3/main or data/features_v3/backgroundThe 10 extra features
5python scripts/run_models.py --features data/features/features.csv data/features_v2/features.csv --extra data/features_v3/main/features.csv data/features_v3/background/features.csv --rule best --out data/results_v3Validation results (about 20 minutes on four cores)
6python scripts/freeze_model.py --results data/results_v3The frozen model
7 (EE)python scripts/gee_export_gujarat.py --project ID --boxes labels/gujarat_boxes.json, and again with --stack extraFeature rasters for data/gujarat/ and data/gujarat_x/
8python scripts/score_gujarat.py --version 3data/gujarat_results_v3/
9python scripts/diagnostics.py, python scripts/alarm_diagnostics.pydata/analysis/
10python scripts/build_site_data.pyThis site, in docs/

Steps 1, 2, 5, 6 and the first command of step 9 were rerun from a clean clone and reproduce the committed files byte for byte. Step 10 can be run with --check, which writes nothing and fails if any page or data file of this site differs from what the result files give.

Data access

  • In the repository: all labels and outlines, all sample points, all four feature tables, all validation and Gujarat results, and the model score of every scored pixel (data/gujarat_results_v3/scores/).
  • Not in the repository: the Gujarat feature rasters, several gigabytes. Step 7 rebuilds them from public data. The image chips on this site were cut from them with scripts/make_chips.py.
  • No account needed: steps 1, 2, 5, 6, 9 (first command) and 10.
  • No private data: the repository holds no credentials or keys. An Earth Engine project identifier is passed on the command line and is not stored.

Verifying the freeze

The order of events can be checked in the history:

git log -1 --format="%h %ad %s" 8711855       # the test list is sealed
git log -1 --format="%h %ad %s" freeze-v3     # the model and rules are frozen
git log --format="%h %ad %s" -- data/gujarat_results_v3/summary.json   # Gujarat is scored

The earlier tags freeze-v1 and freeze-v2 mark the two earlier versions in the same way.

AI-use disclosure

An AI assistant, Claude (Anthropic), was used throughout this project through Claude Code.

  • What the assistant did: wrote the Python scripts in scripts/ to the author's requirements; ran model training, the diagnostics and the reproducibility checks; searched for and summarised related papers; drafted the documentation, the three PDF guides and this website, including its charts and interactive elements; proposed parts of the study design, such as the freeze procedure and the diagnostic analyses, which the author accepted, changed or rejected.
  • What the author did: chose the question; screened every OpenStreetMap candidate and drew every training outline; compiled and outlined the Gujarat test list; ran every Earth Engine fetch and export and the Gujarat scoring on their own machine; judged all 100 alarms in the hand check; made every decision about labels, features, versions and what to report.
  • No label or verdict was produced by AI. The assistant never saw the high-resolution imagery used for labelling and for the hand check.
  • How AI output was checked: results were reproduced from a clean clone; every number on this site is filled from the result files by a script, not typed; the author read the pages against the result files.

Glossary

Alarm patch
A group of touching flagged pixels covering at least 1 ha. It is the unit someone would go and look at.
AUC of one feature
The chance that a randomly chosen landfill pixel has a higher value of that feature than a randomly chosen pixel of another class. 0.5 means the feature carries no information; 0 or 1 means it separates them completely.
Band
One slice of the spectrum measured by a satellite sensor, such as blue, red or near infrared. Each band gives one number per pixel.
BSI
A bare-soil index from four bands. High on bare ground, lower on roofs, so it helps separate the two.
Chip
A small picture cut from the satellite composite, here 1.5 km across, showing what the model sees at 10 m per pixel.
Freeze
The commit in which the model, its threshold and the scoring rules were fixed. It carries a Git tag, pushed before Gujarat was scored.
Gradient boosting
A model built from many small decision trees, each one correcting the errors of those before it.
Hand check
Looking at each of the top alarm patches in high-resolution imagery and recording what is there, without seeing its score or rank.
Held-out region
A validation round in which every place in one region is removed from training and used only for testing. Six rounds, one per region.
Land surface temperature
How warm the ground surface is, measured from thermal infrared. Dumps are usually warmer than their surroundings: dark, dry, and decomposing.
Landsat 8 and 9
US satellites that, among other things, measure how warm the ground is, at about 100 m per pixel. Free.
Leakage
When test rows are near-copies of training rows, for example two pixels of the same dump. The score then measures memory, not skill.
Logistic regression
A model that scores a pixel by a weighted sum of its features. The simplest of the learned models compared here.
Look-alike
A place that is not a dump but resembles one from above: a quarry, an ash pond, bare ground. Used in training as a hard example of what not to flag.
Median composite
For each pixel, the middle value of all its cloud-free observations in a period. It removes clouds, shadows and one-day events.
Model score
The model's output for a pixel, between 0 and 1. A pixel is flagged at 0.5 or above. It is not the probability that the place is a dump.
NDBI
A built-up index from the short-wave and near-infrared bands. High on roofs, paving and bare or disturbed ground.
NDVI
A greenness index from the red and near-infrared bands. High on crops and trees, near zero on bare ground, waste and roofs.
Near-site background
Points drawn at random from the 15 km box around each training landfill. They show the model what the land around a city dump looks like.
Neighbour purity
For one point, the share of its 15 most similar points (from other places) that are landfill. It measures overlap between classes without any model.
Novelty
How far a pixel lies from the training data, as a percentile of the training points' own distances to each other. Above the 99th percentile the model is extrapolating.
Place
Outlines within 500 m of each other are treated as one place, so two cells of the same dump are never split between training and testing.
Pre-monsoon
March to May, the hot dry season before the rains. Skies are clear and fields are mostly bare.
Precision, recall, F1
Recall: of the real landfill pixels, the share flagged. Precision: of the flagged pixels, the share that are landfill. F1 is their harmonic mean.
Prevalence
How common the thing being looked for is. Landfill is 11% of the training points and about 0.02% of the land scored in Gujarat.
Principal components
A way of squeezing many variables into a few new ones that keep as much of the variation as possible, so the data can be drawn in two dimensions.
Random background
Points drawn at random across the states that supply training sites, at least 500 m from any mapped site.
Random forest
A model that averages many decision trees, each trained on a random sample of the data and of the features.
Rank
Alarm patches are ordered by their mean score, strongest first. Rank 1 is the patch the model is most sure of.
Sealed test list
The list of known Gujarat dumpsites with their outlines, committed before any model was run and not used in training.
Sentinel-2
A pair of European satellites that photograph all land every five days in 13 colour bands, at 10 to 20 m per pixel. The data is free.
SHAP
A method that divides one prediction among the features that produced it. Summed over many points it shows what the model relies on.
Site found
A known site counts as found when at least half of the pixels inside its outline are flagged.
SRTM
A global elevation map made from a Space Shuttle radar flight in 2000, at 30 m per pixel.
Surface reflectance
The share of sunlight a surface reflects in one band, from 0 to 1, after the effect of the atmosphere is removed.
Temperature anomaly
A pixel's surface temperature minus the mean of the land within 2 km. It removes regional climate, so places in different states can be compared.
Threshold
The score at or above which a pixel is flagged. Fixed at 0.5 before any result was seen.
Winter season
Mid-November to mid-February, when the winter (rabi) crop is growing and farmland is green.
Zonal Council region
India's states are grouped by law into six zones: Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern and North-Eastern. They are used here as six validation folds.
