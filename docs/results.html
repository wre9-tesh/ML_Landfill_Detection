§ 01

Validation outside Gujarat

With whole regions held out, gradient boosting has the highest F1, 0.762 (precision 0.694, recall 0.865), and was selected by the rule. The published threshold rule reaches 0.415.

The way the data is split changes the answer, and not only its level. Under a random pixel split the random forest scores 0.907 and looks like the clear winner. With regions held out it falls to 0.736, below gradient boosting. A project that validated on random pixels would have chosen a different model and reported a number it could not reproduce in a new state.

Held-out region. A validation round in which every place in one region is removed from training and used only for testing. Six rounds, one per region.

The threshold was fixed at 0.5 before Gujarat was scored. Other values are shown to explain the trade-off; they are not results the project claims. Gujarat values away from 0.50 are computed from scores stored to two decimals, which moves the flagged area by about 1.2%.

Two things are worth trying. At 0.30 all 17 sites are found, and 8.5% of the land is flagged. At 0.70 the model still finds 16 sites and still flags 3.0% of the land. No threshold makes the flagged area small while keeping the sites: the known sites and the false alarms have the same high scores.

§ 03

Gujarat: the known sites

The frozen model found 16 of the 17 sites on the sealed list. The one it missed is Rajkot, where 48.6% of the outline is flagged, just under the half that the rule requires.

Site found. A known site counts as found when at least half of the pixels inside its outline are flagged.

One dumpsite the model was never told about. The Surendranagar landfill is in neither the training data nor the test list. Its box was scored only because the author knew the site was there. The alarm patch on it is rank 4 of 7,801, with a mean score of 0.90, 33 m from the site's centre. It is one example, not a rate.

§ 04

A second blind test: six new cities

The 17 sites above had been scored by two earlier versions of the model. To get a test that no version had touched, the dumpsites of six further cities were found and outlined, and the list was committed (fc81cd5) before the land around them was exported or scored. The frozen model was then run on six new boxes covering 5,244 km², with every rule unchanged.

2 of the new sites stand in the top 50 patches and 3 in the top 100. The site missed is Porbandar, where 18% of the outline is flagged; its extent was uncertain when it was outlined. In these boxes the model flagged 4.0% of the land, much as in the first fourteen. Taking both tests together, the model found 21 of 23 known dumpsites. These sites are small (1 to 6 ha), so the result also shows the model is not limited to large municipal sites. The alarms in the new boxes were not hand-checked.

§ 05

Gujarat: everything else that was flagged

The model flagged 5.2% of the 12,854 km² scored: 666 km², in 7,801 patches of 1 ha or more. The 17 known sites cover 313 ha, 0.4% of the flagged land. There are certainly dumpsites in the boxes that are not on the list, but they cannot amount to hundreds of times the area of the known ones.

Do real dumpsites come first?

Nobody can visit 7,801 places, so what matters is the order. When the known sites are left in the list of patches, 5 of them stand in the top 50, 8 in the top 100 and 9 in the top 200. The other eight are spread down to rank 4,388 of 7,796.

Rank. Alarm patches are ordered by their mean score, strongest first. Rank 1 is the patch the model is most sure of.
§ 06

The hand check of the top 100 alarms

The 100 strongest alarms outside the known outlines were opened one by one. They led to 3 dumpsites that were not known beforehand, one of them just outside Ahmedabad. Two more alarms fell on dumpsites already known to the checker (the Surendranagar landfill, and waste beside a listed site), which makes 5 dumpsites in all. 94 alarms were other kinds of land and 1 could not be decided.

§ 07

Bottom line

The model finds dumpsites it has never seen: 21 of 23 across two sealed lists, one site it was never told about at rank 4, and 3 previously unknown dumpsites among its 100 strongest alarms. Its cost is the checking: it flags about one twentieth of the land, and most of its strongest alarms are bare ground, fields or buildings. The Diagnosis page shows why.
