§ 01

Preprocessing choices and why

DefinitionPre-monsoon

March to May, the hot dry season before the rains. Skies are clear and fields are mostly bare.

ChoiceWhat was doneWhy
SeasonPre-monsoon, 1 March to 31 May 2024Dry and nearly cloud-free; crops are harvested, so bare and waste surfaces are exposed
Second seasonWinter, 15 November 2023 to 14 February 2024Farmland is green in winter and bare before the monsoon. A dumpsite looks the same in both
CompositingPer-pixel median of cloud-masked scenesRemoves cloud, shadow and one-day events such as a fire or a flooded field
Cloud handlingSentinel-2 scenes under 10% cloud, Landsat under 30%; cloud, shadow, cirrus and snow pixels maskedThermal scenes are fewer, so the Landsat limit is looser; cloudy pixels are masked either way
Temperature anomalyTemperature minus the mean of land within 2 km, permanent water left outRemoves regional climate, so a site in Rajasthan and one in Kerala are comparable. Leaving out water stops coastal sites from looking hot
SurroundingsMeans within 150 m and standard deviations within 50 mOne pixel cannot tell waste from a roof or a bare plot; what is around it might
ResolutionEvery input sampled at 10 mThe finest input. Coarser inputs are resampled, which does not sharpen them
Points per placeAt most 50 per landfill place, 30 per look-alike placeOne 300 ha site must not outweigh thirty small ones
Edge insetPoints at least 15 m inside the outline and 15 m apartEdge pixels mix waste with what surrounds it; no pixel is sampled twice
BackgroundRandom points at least 500 m from any mapped site, plus points in the 15 km boxes around training landfillsRandom points show ordinary India. Near-site points show the land around a city dump, which is what the Gujarat boxes contain
Missing valuesA row with any missing feature is dropped2.8% of points; nothing imputed, so nothing to explain away
ScalingStandardised inside the logistic-regression pipeline onlyTree models do not need it
Class weightsBalancedLandfill is 11% of the points
DefinitionTemperature anomaly

A pixel's surface temperature minus the mean of the land within 2 km. It removes regional climate, so places in different states can be compared.

DefinitionGradient boosting

A model built from many small decision trees, each one correcting the errors of those before it.

All learned models use scikit-learn defaults with balanced class weights, a threshold of 0.5 and random seed 678. Nothing was tuned.

Why no tuning: with 86 places in six folds, tuned settings would fit the folds. Fixed defaults keep the comparison between models fair and leave nothing to adjust after seeing a result.
Why a threshold rule is the baseline: it is the method of the study that prompted the project. If three thresholds were enough, nothing more complicated would be justified.
§ 03

Three ways to split the data

The same models were validated three ways. Only the third resembles the real task.

DefinitionLeakage

When test rows are near-copies of training rows, for example two pixels of the same dump. The score then measures memory, not skill.

  • Random pixel. Points are assigned to folds at random, so pixels from the same dumpsite land on both sides. A model can score well by recognising the site, not the class.
  • Held-out site. Whole places are held out. Neighbouring sites in the same city or state remain in training.
  • Held-out region (the headline). Every place in one of India's six Zonal Council regions is held out in turn. The model is tested on a part of the country it has not seen, which is what scoring Gujarat demands.
DefinitionZonal Council region

India's states are grouped by law into six zones: Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern and North-Eastern. They are used here as six validation folds.

Why regions and not states: the plan was to hold out one state at a time, for states with at least ten places. Only Maharashtra has that many, so the fallback written into the plan applied: six regions of 5 to 23 places each.
§ 04

Metrics and the selection rule

DefinitionPrecision, recall, F1

Recall: of the real landfill pixels, the share flagged. Precision: of the flagged pixels, the share that are landfill. F1 is their harmonic mean.

For each held-out region: precision, recall and F1 for the landfill class at a threshold of 0.5. They are reported as the mean and standard deviation over the six regions. The share of each background class that is flagged is reported alongside, because a single precision figure hides which kind of land causes the false alarms.

The model with the highest mean F1 on the held-out-region split is selected. This rule was written down before the models were run.

§ 05

The freeze

A test is only a test if nothing can be adjusted after seeing it. Three things were fixed in the repository's history, in this order:

  1. 11 November. The Gujarat test list was committed (8711855), before any model existed.
  2. 15 November. The selected model, its threshold and every scoring rule were committed and tagged freeze-v3. The rules are in docs/freeze_v3.md, including what would count as an improvement.
  3. After the tag. Gujarat was scored. The scoring script refuses to run unless the model file is the tagged one.
DefinitionFreeze

The commit in which the model, its threshold and the scoring rules were fixed. It carries a Git tag, pushed before Gujarat was scored.

This is the third model built in this project. Two earlier versions were frozen and scored on the same 17 sites, so the Gujarat results are not a first-time test. See How we got here.

§ 06

Scoring Gujarat

DefinitionModel score

The model's output for a pixel, between 0 and 1. A pixel is flagged at 0.5 or above. It is not the probability that the place is a dump.

Scored area
14 boxes, each reaching 15 km beyond the known sites of one city: 12,854 km², 129 million pixels. One box, Surendranagar, surrounds a dumpsite that is in neither the training data nor the test list.
Flagged pixel
A pixel with a score of 0.5 or more.
Site found
At least half of the pixels inside the site's outline are flagged.
Alarm patch
Touching flagged pixels outside the known outlines, covering at least 1 ha.
Ranking
Patches are ordered by their mean score, strongest first.
§ 07

The hand check

Recall on 17 known sites says nothing about the thousands of other places the model flags. To measure that, the 100 strongest alarm patches were given random identifiers and opened one at a time in Google Earth, with scores and ranks hidden. Each was recorded as a dump, a look-alike or unsure, with a note of what was there.

DefinitionHand check

Looking at each of the top alarm patches in high-resolution imagery and recording what is there, without seeing its score or rank.

One hundred is enough to estimate a proportion to within about ten percentage points. The check was done by the author alone, from imagery, which makes it the weakest link in the evaluation: a second checker or a field visit could disagree.
