1The project at a glance

The project asks whether a model that has never seen Gujarat can find Gujarat's landfills and dumpsites from free satellite data. The model is trained only on Indian sites outside Gujarat. Every choice is then frozen in a tagged commit, and the frozen model is tested on Gujarat once.

There are four deliverables: a four-page paper in the course LaTeX template, the repository (reproducible from a clean clone), the depth analysis required by the graduate track, and a presentation website that replaces the viva. If time runs short, the paper comes first.

2The question and the design

Research questions

  1. Transfer. Does a model trained outside Gujarat find Gujarat's dumpsites?
  2. Validation design. How much does the score change as the test gets harder: random pixel, then held-out site, then held-out state, then Gujarat?
  3. Rarity. What precision does the model have when dumpsites are rare? The proposal's example is a published 93% on a balanced test, which becomes about 12% precision if 1 pixel in 100 is a dumpsite.

Why the design looks the way it does

Most published landfill detectors are tested on pixels drawn from the same sites, or the same region, that they were trained on. That measures memory of those places more than the ability to find new ones. This project is built around three safeguards against that.

  • A held-out state. Nothing from Gujarat is ever used in training. The Gujarat list was sealed in its own commit (8711855) before any model was run on it.
  • Rules fixed before results. The features, the nine models, the validation folds and the rule for choosing the primary model were written down on 4 and 9 November, before any model was trained. No choice can be bent towards a number that has already been seen.
  • A freeze. The chosen model, threshold and scoring rules are committed and tagged, and the tag is pushed, before Gujarat is scored. The tag's GitHub timestamp is the evidence that the test was run once.

The consequence is that a poor Gujarat result is still a valid result. It is written up as it is; nothing is retuned afterwards.

3Step 1: labels

Labels are the one input nothing downstream can fix. Three sets were built: landfill sites outside Gujarat (positives), look-alikes and background outside Gujarat (negatives), and the sealed Gujarat test list.

3.1 OpenStreetMap candidates

How. Every object tagged landuse=landfill in India was exported from OpenStreetMap (OSM) with an Overpass query on 3 November. scripts/osm_to_screening.py turned the 917 objects into a screening list.

StageCountNote

Inside Gujarat47Set aside; used only to help build the test list
Training candidates857416 of them 1 ha or larger

Why OSM. It is free, covers the whole country, and gives outlines, not just pins. Its weakness is that the tag is used loosely: many of the largest "landfills" are fly-ash ponds, mine spoil or ore yards. That is why every site is checked by eye.

3.2 Screening by eye

How. Each candidate was opened in Google Earth Pro with historical imagery set as close as possible to March–May 2024, the window the satellite features come from. The decision went into labels/india_screening.csv: keep (active dumping visible), drop with a reason, or unsure.

Why the 2024 window. A label must describe the ground at the time the satellite measured it. A site capped in 2022 or opened in 2025 would teach the model the wrong thing.

How. You drew 82 outlines of known municipal dumpsites outside Gujarat in Google Earth Pro (the Extra_Sites folder). scripts/kml_to_csv.py converted them to labels/extra_sites.csv and .geojson, and scripts/build_samples.py merged them into places.

Why merge, and why 1 ha

Two outlines 100 m apart are one landfill. Counting them twice would let the same site sit on both sides of a validation split. The 1 ha minimum exists because 1 ha is about one native Landsat thermal pixel (100 m), and because a smaller outline leaves fewer than 20 sample points once the 15 m edge inset is applied.

3.4 Labelling rules

  • A site counts if active dumping is visible in March–May 2024 imagery. If only 2023 and 2025 images exist, it is kept when active in both, and only the area that is waste in both is outlined.
  • The 1 ha minimum is measured on the visible waste, not on the compound wall.
  • Biomining areas count while waste is still there. Finished, levelled ground does not.
  • Small green patches stay inside an outline. Large ones are routed around or split off.
  • A site in both OSM and your list is entered once.
  • Look-alike classes: quarry (includes mines and spoil), kiln, bare soil, construction, riverbed, ash pond, industrial (slag, coal, stockyards).

3.5 Negatives

The model needs examples of what is not a landfill. Two kinds are used, for different reasons.

  • Look-alikes are places a detector is likely to confuse with a dump: ash ponds, bare soil and quarries. They come from the OSM rows dropped during screening, so each one was checked by eye. They make the training problem hard in the right way, and their class column drives the failure audit later.
  • Random background points are drawn anywhere in the states that supply sites. They represent ordinary land: fields, forest, towns, water.

3.6 The sealed Gujarat test list

How. You marked Gujarat dumpsites with pins and outlines in labels/Gujarat_landfills_ml_test.kml. Outlines that share a name (for example the two parts of AHM-02) are parts of one site. The converted list, labels/gujarat_test_sites.csv and .geojson, was committed on its own as the seal.

Result. 17 outlined sites in 13 cities, all at least 1 ha, from 1.1 ha (Dwarka) to 107.5 ha (Surat, Khajod). One more site, Surat LF-3, has a pin and no outline, so it cannot be scored. The target was 20 or more.

Why sealed. If the list could change after the model's map is seen, sites the model happened to find could be added and missed ones quietly dropped. A separate, timestamped commit removes that possibility.

5Step 3: features

scripts/gee_features.py reads twelve values at every point from Google Earth Engine. All come from the 2024 pre-monsoon window, 1 March to 31 May 2024, when cloud is low and vegetation is at its driest.

Why medians. A median over three months removes clouds, shadows and one-off events that a single image would carry.

Why BSI and B2 were added late. They were added on 9 November, before any feature was fetched or any model run, so the addition could not be tuned to results.

Outcome. The full fetch ran on 11 November. All 9,530 points were fetched; 60 random points came back without Sentinel-2 values (snow in Uttarakhand, Ladakh and Jammu and Kashmir) and are left out, leaving 9,470.

6Step 4: the nine models

All nine were fixed on 4 November. They use scikit-learn defaults except where stated, class-balanced weights, and a 0.5 threshold. There is no hyperparameter tuning. scripts/run_models.py runs them.

ModelSettingsWhy it is in the comparison

Decision treeFully grownSingle-tree reference: low bias, high variance

AdaBoost200 stumps, SAMMEBoosting with the weakest learner
Gradient boosting100 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.1The strongest standard tabular method
Why no tuning, and why class weights

Tuning nine models needs a second layer of validation, and with six folds it would mostly fit noise. Defaults make the comparison about the method, not about search effort. Class-balanced weights make landfill and non-landfill points count equally in training, so all models can be compared at the same 0.5 threshold.

Two details the checklist left open were settled in the script before any real result: the recalibrated rule takes the cut-offs with the best training F1, searched over each feature's 5th to 95th percentiles; and "one standard deviation" in the selection rule is the sample SD across folds.

7Step 5: validation and selection

Three splits

SplitWhat is held outWhat it measures
Random pixelA random fifth of all points, five timesAlmost nothing new: other pixels of the same landfill are in training
Held-out siteA fifth of the places, five times; every point of a place stays togetherNew sites in regions the model already knows
Held-out region (headline)One whole region at a timeNew sites in a region the model has never seen: the closest rehearsal for Gujarat

Why regions and not states

The rule fixed on 4 November was leave-one-state-out, with a state needing at least 10 kept places to be its own fold, and a fallback to the Zonal Council regions if fewer than five states qualified. Only Maharashtra (15 places) qualifies, so the fallback applies. The grouping was fixed in advance, so it was not chosen after seeing results.

Metrics

  • Precision: of the points the model flags, the share that are landfill.
  • Recall: of the landfill points, the share the model flags.
  • F1: the harmonic mean of the two; it is high only when both are.

All three are reported per fold as well as on average, because the spread between folds is a result in itself.

Selection rule

The primary model is the one with the best mean held-out-region F1, unless a simpler model is within one standard deviation of it; then the simplest such model is taken. The order from simplest is: published rule, recalibrated rule, logistic regression, decision tree, bagged trees, random forest, AdaBoost, gradient boosting.

Why. With six folds, a difference smaller than the fold-to-fold spread is not evidence that one model is better. Between models that cannot be told apart, the simpler one is easier to explain, to audit and to apply elsewhere.

False alarms and what they imply

Pooled over the held-out rounds, logistic regression flags 89.0% of landfill points and 5.1% of random background points. Among look-alikes it flags 20% of ash-pond points, 39% of bare-soil points and 43% of quarry points.

The validation data is about 41% landfill, which is nothing like the real landscape. Applying the same two rates to realistic shares gives the answer to research question 3:

A model with 83% precision on the balanced validation set would be wrong about most of what it flags on a real map. This is why the output is called a model score and not a probability, and why the Gujarat pass scores every pixel instead of a balanced sample.

9Step 6: the freeze

What it is. One commit that contains the final labels, the points, the features, the results, the saved model and the scoring rules, with a tag (freeze-v1) pushed to GitHub before Gujarat is scored.

How. After the last rerun, scripts/freeze_model.py fits the selected model on all training points and saves data/results/model.joblib and model.json. Those are committed, the commit is tagged, and the tag is pushed. scripts/score_gujarat.py refuses to run on a commit that has no freeze tag.

Why write the failure criterion first

Deciding what failure means after seeing the result is how a negative result gets reframed as a success. A sentence written beforehand makes the conclusion checkable.

10Step 7: scoring Gujarat

10.1 The scoring area

The decision on 11 November was to score a box 15 km beyond the test sites of each test city, and to score locally. scripts/gee_export_gujarat.py exports the same twelve-band stack the training points were sampled from, one GeoTIFF per city at 10 m in UTM zone 43N. You started the 13 exports on 11 November; they land in the Drive folder landfill_gujarat and go into data/gujarat/.

The 13 boxes hold about 125 million pixels, roughly 12,500 km². Exporting features reveals no result, so doing it before the freeze is safe.

Why local scoring. It works for whichever model the rule picks, including boosted models that are awkward to rebuild inside Earth Engine, and it keeps the scored rasters as files the website can use.

Why whole boxes. Scoring only around known sites would hide the false alarms. The boxes contain cities, farmland, industry, quarries and coast at their real proportions, which is the real-rarity test.

10.2 What the scoring script does

  1. Applies the frozen model to every pixel that has all twelve features, and writes a score map per city.
  2. Counts a pixel that lies in two overlapping boxes once.
  3. For each sealed site, reports the pixels inside its outline, the share flagged, the highest score and whether it counts as found.
  4. Groups flagged pixels outside the sealed outlines into patches, keeps those of at least 1 ha, and ranks them by mean score.
  5. Writes the top 100 as alarms_blind.kml, pins named only by a shuffled number.

10.3 The blind check

You open the blind KML in Google Earth Pro and record each pin as dumpsite, look-alike (with its class) or unclear, without knowing its rank or score. That gives precision at the top of the ranking, the number that matters to anyone who would act on the map.

Why blind. Knowing that a pin is the model's top pick makes it easier to see a dump there.

10.4 What Gujarat produces

  • Site recall against the sealed list, with the per-site flagged share.
  • The share of all scored pixels that are flagged.
  • Precision among the top 100 alarms from the blind check.
  • The fourth rung of the split ablation.

12Step 9: paper, website, repository

Four pages including references, two columns, in the course template. Draft order: methods, results, limitations, discussion, introduction, abstract. Website

A static site on GitHub Pages built from the frozen outputs, with no backend. 15Known limitations

  • Few sites. 86 landfill places is enough to train on but gives small folds. The North-Eastern fold has 5 places, so its numbers are noisy.
  • Hand-picked positives. 75 of 86 places are sites you already knew or could find: mostly large, well-known municipal dumps. The model may be weaker on small or informal ones.
  • Three look-alike classes. Only ash ponds, bare soil and quarries are represented. There are no kilns, construction sites, riverbeds or industrial yards, so the model has not been taught to reject those.
  • Most OSM rows unscreened. 736 of 843 were never looked at. They are kept out of the background sample, but they are not used either.
  • Background may hide dumps. Random points avoid mapped sites by 500 m, but an unmapped dump could still be sampled as background.
  • Short test list. 17 scoreable Gujarat sites against a target of 20. With 17 sites, one site is 6 percentage points of recall.
  • Coarse temperature. Landsat's thermal band is about 100 m; it is resampled to 10 m, so small sites share their temperature with their surroundings.
  • Untested scripts. freeze_model.py and score_gujarat.py have run only on made-up rasters. The first real export may expose a problem, for example in band names or file tiling.
